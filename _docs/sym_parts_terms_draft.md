# Draft Points — New SYM Parts Terms & Conditions

**Purpose:** a content brief for a new customer-facing “New SYM Parts” terms
section, to sit alongside the existing purchase terms. This is not legal advice
and should be reviewed by an Australian consumer-law adviser before publishing.

The tone should match the existing ScooterShop purchase terms: plain English,
numbered sections, and clear contact details for FEPS (WA) PTY LTD.

---

## Proposed structure and points

### 1. Scope and who is selling

- These terms apply to genuine new SYM parts ordered through ScooterShop.
- Identify the seller as **FEPS (WA) PTY LTD**, including ABN, address, phone and
  email, consistent with the existing terms.
- Clarify that ScooterShop sells the parts to the customer; Select Scooters/
  Select Portal is the fulfilment supplier, not the customer’s contracting party.

### 2. Parts identification and customer responsibility

- The customer uses the model, exploded diagram, callout number, part number,
  colour and running-change information to select their parts.
- The customer should check the selected part number, quantity, colour and
  fitment before paying.
- We can help with a query before purchase, but should not promise compatibility
  where the supplied information is incomplete or inaccurate.
- Do **not** phrase this as removing consumer rights. It is an accuracy and
  fitment-check reminder only.

### 3. Pricing, GST and payment

- All displayed customer prices are in Australian dollars and include GST unless
  clearly stated otherwise.
- The checkout total includes the parts price plus the Australian shipping charge.
- Prices and availability are rechecked at checkout; if a part is no longer
  available or its price has changed, the customer must be told before payment
  can proceed.
- Payment is processed through Stripe. An order reference is provided after an
  order is created.
- Reserve the right to correct an obvious pricing or catalogue error before the
  order is fulfilled, while preserving all rights required by law.

### 4. Availability and backorders

- If one or more parts are unavailable, we will email a line-by-line update.
- We will hold the order for up to **7 days** while the unavailable part is
  expected to return to stock.
- If a part cannot be obtained in that period, we may refund that line and
  arrange shipment of the remaining available parts.
- Delivery dates and supplier ETAs are estimates, not guarantees, except where
  consumer law requires otherwise.

### 5. Supplier fulfilment and delivery

- Parts are normally shipped directly from the supplier to the delivery address
  entered at checkout.
- The customer must provide a complete and accurate delivery address, contact
  details and any required delivery instructions.
- Set out whether authority-to-leave, redelivery fees, address changes after
  dispatch and PO boxes are supported. These are still business decisions to make.
- Clearly disclose that delivery timing can depend on supplier processing and
  the carrier. Avoid promising a dispatch or delivery timeframe until one is
  genuinely supportable.
- Delivery is currently available to Australian addresses only.

### 6. Order-arranged communications

- Once the supplier order has been arranged, ScooterShop may email the customer
  to confirm this.
- “Arranged” means the supplier order has been placed; it should not be worded
  as a carrier-tracking or guaranteed-delivery notice unless tracking is actually
  available.

### 7. Cancellations, returns and wrong selections

- State the change-of-mind position for correctly supplied parts. A sensible
  proposed policy is that returns need prior approval and parts must be unused,
  uninstalled, undamaged and in original packaging, but only adopt this after
  confirming the supplier’s return policy and any restocking cost.
- State that special-order, electrical, opened, fitted or damaged parts may not
  be eligible for a change-of-mind return where permitted by law.
- Explain how a customer requests a return: email ScooterShop with the `SP-...`
  reference, part number, reason and photos if relevant; do not send a part back
  until return instructions are provided.
- Make clear that a customer selecting an incorrect part does not remove their
  Australian Consumer Law rights.

### 8. Faulty, damaged or incorrect goods

- Ask customers to contact ScooterShop promptly with their order reference,
  description of the issue and photos of transit damage where practical.
- ScooterShop handles the customer’s remedy; customers should not be told to
  deal directly with the supplier or manufacturer.
- Include the required Australian Consumer Law goods guarantee wording, or link
  clearly to the version approved for the site:

  > Our goods come with guarantees that cannot be excluded under the Australian
  > Consumer Law. You are entitled to a replacement or refund for a major
  > failure and compensation for any other reasonably foreseeable loss or
  > damage. You are also entitled to have the goods repaired or replaced if the
  > goods fail to be of acceptable quality and the failure does not amount to a
  > major failure.

### 9. Refunds

- A refund for an unavailable line is processed to the original payment method
  after the decision to cancel that line.
- Say that bank processing can take up to **14 days** after we process the
  refund.
- Decide and state the treatment of shipping when all lines are cancelled versus
  only some lines are cancelled. Proposed starting point:
  - partial refund: refund unavailable part line(s); shipping remains payable if
    the remaining order is shipped;
  - full non-supply refund: refund all amounts paid, including shipping.
- Do not label a line “refunded” or send the refund email until the Stripe refund
  has actually been processed.

### 10. Australian Consumer Law and liability

- State that nothing in the terms excludes, restricts or modifies rights or
  remedies that cannot lawfully be excluded, including the Australian Consumer
  Law consumer guarantees.
- Any limitation of liability must be legally reviewed; do not rely on a broad
  “no consequential loss” clause alone to avoid consumer obligations.

### 11. Privacy, contact and changes to terms

- Customer details are used to process payment, communicate about the order and
  provide the ship-to details necessary for supplier drop-shipping.
- Refer to the Privacy Policy if/when one is linked in the footer.
- Provide the correct contact email, phone and business address.
- The terms version accepted at checkout should be saved with the order or be
  otherwise recoverable. Future updates should apply only to new orders.

## Decisions needed before drafting final legal copy

1. Is the published backorder promise always 7 calendar days, or business days?
2. What is the precise change-of-mind return policy and who bears return postage?
3. Will the supplier accept returns for fitted, opened, electrical or special
   order parts, and is there a restocking fee?
4. What happens to the shipping charge on a full cancellation, partial refund
   and supplier-caused delivery failure?
5. Will a dispatch/tracking notice be available later, or is “order arranged”
   the final automated delivery communication for MVP?
6. Should an order become binding on payment, supplier acceptance, or another
   clearly stated point? Obtain legal advice before using an “acceptance” clause.
